Multimodal Reasoning and Agents

By Chaofan Tao (陶超凡) · Last updated November 3, 2026

This work studies how vision-language models reason, search and act. It covers reflection-aware RL for multimodal reasoning (SRPO), the interplay of long chain-of-thought SFT and RL in reasoning VLMs, physics-grounded evaluation (PhyX), provenance-aware multimodal browsing agents (MMSearch-Plus), compositional video-language understanding (NAVERO), and multimodal code generation for robot behavior (RoboCodeX).

Key results

Does RL or long-CoT SFT make vision-language models reason better?

They help in different places, and combining them does not simply add their gains. Long chain-of-thought SFT improves hard questions through structured reasoning but makes answers verbose and hurts easy questions. RL improves every difficulty level and keeps answers short, with smaller gains on the hardest problems. Two-stage, interleaved and progressive schedules, data mixing and model merging all land on a trade-off instead of a sum, which the paper calls the synergy dilemma.

How can multimodal LLMs learn to self-reflect?

SRPO first builds a reflection-focused dataset in which a stronger MLLM critiques initial answers, so the policy learns both reasoning and reflection. It then adds a GRPO reward that favors short, meaningful reflection over redundant self-talk.

Can multimodal models reason about physics?

Not yet at human level. PhyX spans thermodynamics, electromagnetism, mechanics, modern physics, optics, and waves and acoustics. Failure analysis points to over-reliance on memorized formulas and surface-level visual pattern matching rather than physical understanding.

What makes multimodal search agents fail?

Locating the right web page and telling apart visually similar events. MMSearch-Plus builds questions whose answers depend on spatial and temporal cues inside an image, and gives agents a set-of-mark tool to crop, zoom and launch targeted searches.

From multimodal reasoning to embodied agents

RoboCodeX decomposes instructions into object-centric manipulation units with affordance and safety constraints and expresses them as code, which lets one model generalize across robot platforms. PhyX measures the physical reasoning such agents need, and the survey of autoregressive models in vision (TMLR 2025, about 250 references, 800+ GitHub stars) covers applications including embodied AI.

FAQ

Does combining long chain-of-thought SFT and RL help reasoning VLMs?

Not additively. The Synergy Dilemma study (TMLR 2025) finds that long-CoT SFT helps on hard questions but adds verbosity and hurts easy ones, while RL improves all difficulty levels and shortens answers. Two-stage, interleaved and progressive training, data mixing and model merging all trade off accuracy, reasoning style and length instead of adding the benefits.

What is PhyX?

PhyX is a benchmark of 3K multimodal questions on physics-grounded reasoning, covering 6 physics domains and 25 sub-domains. GPT-4o, Claude 3.7 Sonnet and o4-mini reach 32.5%, 42.2% and 45.8%, more than 29 points below human experts.

What is MMSearch-Plus?

MMSearch-Plus is a 311-task benchmark for multimodal browsing agents that requires extracting fine-grained visual cues and cross-validating them through image and text search. The strongest system reaches 36.0% end-to-end accuracy, and the set-of-mark module adds up to 3.9 points.

Does Chaofan Tao work on embodied AI?

His main focus is coding agents and efficient LLMs. Embodied-related contributions include RoboCodeX (ICML 2024), a multimodal code generation framework for robotic behavior synthesis evaluated in simulation and on real robots, and PhyX, a physical reasoning benchmark for multimodal models.
